What Does FTD Mean on a Paystub? Fiscal Year to Date vs Federal Tax Deposit

FTD has two meanings in payroll. On an employee stub it usually means fiscal year to date; in an employer's records it means federal tax deposit. How to tell which you are looking at.

By Alexander Caldwell · October 13, 2026 · 1 min read

Key takeaways

  • On a pay stub, FTD most often means fiscal year to date.
  • Fiscal year to date tracks a cumulative total across the employer's fiscal year, not the calendar year.
  • It can differ from YTD whenever the employer's fiscal year does not start in January.
  • In employer records, FTD means federal tax deposit, the payment of withheld taxes to the IRS.
  • Your W-2 is built on calendar-year totals, so YTD is the figure to reconcile against it.

FTD is an abbreviation that genuinely means two different things in payroll, and which one applies depends on whose document you are reading.

On Your Pay Stub: Fiscal Year to Date

Most often FTD on an employee statement means fiscal year to date: the cumulative total since the start of the employer's fiscal year. If that fiscal year begins in January, FTD and YTD will match. If the organisation runs a fiscal year starting in July or October, as many schools, universities and public bodies do, the two columns diverge.

That divergence is the whole reason the column exists. The employer wants totals aligned to its own budget year, while tax reporting needs calendar-year totals.

In Employer Records: Federal Tax Deposit

On the employer side, FTD means federal tax deposit: the payment of withheld income tax plus both shares of Social Security and Medicare to the IRS, usually through the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System. Deposits follow a monthly or semiweekly schedule set by the employer's prior liability. An employee will not normally see this on a stub, but it appears in payroll reports and accounting records.

Which Column to Trust for Tax

Use YTD. Your W-2 is built from calendar-year totals, so when reconciling a wage statement against your year-end form, the year-to-date column is the one that should agree. If your stub shows an FTD column and the figures look unexpectedly low or high, check the fiscal year start before assuming an error.

I explain both meanings and how to tell them apart on an unfamiliar stub in my explainer on FTD for Online Paystub.

When in Doubt, Ask

Payroll labels are not standardised and employers can define custom columns. If a cumulative figure on your stub does not reconcile with anything you recognise, ask payroll which period it covers. The answer is usually a fiscal year boundary rather than a mistake.
