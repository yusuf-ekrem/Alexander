How to Cancel a Pending Transaction on a Credit Card

A pending charge is an authorisation hold, not a completed payment, and your card issuer usually cannot remove it. Here is who can, how long holds last and when to dispute instead.

By Alexander Caldwell · September 12, 2026 · 2 min read

Key takeaways

  • A pending transaction is an authorisation hold placed by the merchant, not a settled charge.
  • Card issuers generally cannot cancel a pending charge; the merchant has to release it.
  • Holds typically fall off within a few business days if the merchant never captures them.
  • Hotels, car rentals and fuel stations place holds larger than the final amount.
  • Once a charge posts, the route is a dispute under the Fair Credit Billing Act, not a cancellation.

  2. Go to the Merchant First

You see a charge you did not expect, it says pending, and the instinct is to call the card issuer and have it stopped. That call rarely produces the outcome people want, because of how card authorisations actually work.

What Pending Actually Means

When you pay, the merchant asks your issuer to authorise the amount. The issuer sets those funds aside, reducing your available credit, and the transaction shows as pending. No money has moved. Settlement happens later, often a day or several, when the merchant captures the authorisation.

Because the merchant controls the capture, the merchant is the party who can release a hold. The issuer is holding funds at the merchant's request and generally will not unilaterally reverse that request.

Go to the Merchant First

If the purchase was a mistake, a duplicate or something you have already cancelled, contact the merchant and ask them to void the authorisation rather than process a refund. A void removes the hold and nothing ever posts. A refund, by contrast, lets the charge settle and then sends money back, which means both entries appear on your statement and your available credit stays reduced for longer.

Ask for written confirmation. If the hold does not clear, that confirmation is what you will need next.

Holds That Are Supposed to Be Larger

Hotels, car rental companies and fuel pumps routinely authorise more than the expected total to cover incidentals. These are not errors. The excess is released after the final amount is captured, though the timing depends on your issuer. Knowing this prevents a lot of unnecessary phone calls.

Unauthorised charges are a different matter and should be reported to your issuer promptly, since card networks and federal rules give you protections there. I set out the timelines, the void-versus-refund distinction and the dispute route in my guide to cancelling a pending credit card transaction for Online Paystub.

When It Posts Anyway

If the charge settles and the merchant will not resolve it, you move from cancellation to dispute. The Fair Credit Billing Act gives cardholders the right to dispute billing errors on credit card accounts within a defined window, so act rather than wait. Keep your correspondence with the merchant; it is the evidence the dispute turns on.
