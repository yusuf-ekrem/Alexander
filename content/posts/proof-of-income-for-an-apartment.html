Rental applications turn on one calculation: can this applicant afford the rent reliably? The common benchmark is gross monthly income of about three times the monthly rent, though the multiple varies by market and landlord. Proof of income is how you demonstrate you clear it.

The Documents That Usually Satisfy It

  • Pay stubs, typically the two or three most recent. These are the default because they show gross pay, pay frequency and year-to-date earnings in one place.
  • An employment verification letter on company letterhead, stating your position, start date and salary. Useful when you have just started and stubs do not yet reflect the new income.
  • The prior year's W-2 or tax return, which evidences a full year of earnings.
  • Bank statements showing regular deposits, often requested alongside rather than instead of the above.
  • An offer letter, for applicants relocating before the first payday.

Self-Employed and Contract Applicants

Without an employer issuing stubs, the burden shifts to your own records. Filed tax returns covering one or two years, 1099 forms from clients, bank statements and a profit-and-loss statement together build the same picture. Expect the review to take longer, and offer the documents proactively rather than waiting to be asked twice.

Prepare Before You Apply

Competitive rentals move quickly, and the applicant with a complete file often wins over one who is still gathering documents. Download recent stubs while you have portal access, save a PDF of last year's tax return, and have the verification letter requested before you start viewings.

One Warning

Landlords and screening services verify what they receive, often by contacting the employer directly. Submitting a fabricated or altered pay stub is fraud, and the consequences extend well beyond a declined application. If your income genuinely falls short of the threshold, a co-signer, a larger deposit or a smaller unit are the legitimate routes forward.
