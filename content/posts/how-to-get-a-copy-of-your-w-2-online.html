A missing W-2 is usually a retrieval problem rather than a tax problem. The wages were earned, the employer filed the form, and a copy exists somewhere. The question is which route reaches it fastest.

Start With the Payroll Portal

Most employers now deliver W-2s electronically through the same self-service system that holds your pay stubs. If you still have credentials, this is the quickest option and it gives you the complete form, including state and local boxes. Many providers keep documents available to former employees for a period after separation, so it is worth trying even if you have moved on.

Search your email for payroll notifications if you cannot remember which provider your employer used; the account setup message usually identifies it.

Ask the Employer Directly

If the portal is closed to you, contact the employer's payroll or HR team and request a reissued copy. Confirm the address or email they hold for you, since a form sent to an old address is a common reason one never arrived. Employers are subject to payroll recordkeeping requirements, so the record exists even when your access does not.

The IRS Transcript Route

Taxpayers with an IRS Online Account may be able to view wage and income transcript information, which reflects the data employers reported. This is genuinely useful for prior years when an employer has closed or stopped responding.

It is not a perfect substitute. A transcript does not include the state and local tax information printed on the original form, and current-year data can be incomplete until the employer's filing has been processed. For a state return, you may still need the actual document.

If the Deadline Is Close

A missing form does not excuse the wages from being reported. The IRS provides a substitute wage statement for qualifying situations where an employer does not furnish a W-2, and an accurate final pay stub becomes important evidence for completing it. If the real form arrives later and differs from what you filed, an amended return may be required, so keep both documents together.
