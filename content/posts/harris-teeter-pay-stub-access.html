Large grocery employers moved away from paper stubs years ago. Pay is deposited electronically and the statement that explains it sits in an employee self-service portal. The mechanics are similar across major retailers, Harris Teeter included, even though the branding of the portal differs.

Where the Statement Lives

When you were hired you were issued credentials for the employer's self-service system. That system typically holds your pay statements, tax documents, benefit elections and personal details. Pay stubs are usually posted on or just before each pay date and remain available for a defined retention period.

If you have never signed in, start with the onboarding email or ask your store manager. Do not create an account on a third-party website that claims to retrieve stubs for you; the only legitimate route is the employer's own system.

What to Check When You Open It

Retail schedules vary week to week, which makes the earnings section worth more than a glance. Confirm the pay period, the hours recorded, your rate, any overtime or premium pay, then the deductions and the resulting net pay. Finish on the year-to-date column, because that is where a missing shift or a mis-posted adjustment shows up.

Keep Your Own Copies

This is the part employees skip and later regret. Portal access is a privilege of employment and it generally ends at some point after you leave. Download a PDF of each statement, or at minimum save the final stub of each year, and store them somewhere you control. Lenders and landlords ask for recent stubs, and reconstructing them after access is gone is far harder than saving them as they arrive.

If You Have Already Left

Contact the employer's HR or payroll department and ask how former employees request copies of wage statements. Employers are subject to payroll recordkeeping requirements, so the records exist even when your login no longer works. Ask specifically for the pay periods you need and confirm the address or email they will send them to.
