Getting a W-2 from a previous employer usually starts with the employer itself. Check any payroll portal you used while employed, then contact the former employer's payroll or HR team and confirm that they have your current mailing address. If the form is still missing after you have tried the employer, the IRS provides additional options.

A W-2 is an official wage and tax statement. It reports wages paid by an employer and certain taxes withheld during the year. Leaving a job does not make the form irrelevant. If you worked for an employer during the tax year, that employer's W-2 information may still be needed when you prepare your federal income tax return.

Start With Your Former Employer

Your former employer is generally the best first source for a missing W-2. Ask whether the form has already been issued, whether it was mailed or delivered electronically and which address or email account was used.

If you moved after leaving the company, verify your current address. A W-2 may have been sent to an address that was correct while you were employed but is no longer current. If the company uses a third-party payroll provider, HR may direct you to a former-employee portal or explain how to request a reissued copy.

Check Your Old Payroll Portal and Email

Many payroll systems keep tax documents available online even after an employee leaves, although access policies differ by employer. Search your email for old payroll notifications, account setup messages and tax-document notices. These can help you identify the payroll provider and the correct login page.

If your access has been disabled, contact the employer rather than creating a new account with different information. Payroll or HR can tell you how former employees are expected to recover their documents.

When Should a W-2 Arrive?

W-2 forms are generally furnished around the end of January following the tax year. If you changed jobs during the year, you may receive a separate W-2 from each employer that paid you wages.

This is why it helps to make a simple list of the employers you worked for before filing. If you expect three W-2 forms and only two arrive, you can identify the missing form before preparing an incomplete return.

What to Do If the W-2 Is Still Missing

If you do not receive the form, contact the employer first. If you have already done that and the W-2 is still unavailable by the end of February, current IRS guidance allows you to contact the IRS for assistance.

Keep useful information nearby when you contact the IRS, including your identifying information, the former employer's name and address and the dates you worked there. The IRS may contact the employer and can provide instructions involving Form 4852, Substitute for Form W-2, Wage and Tax Statement.

Can You Use an IRS Transcript Instead?

An IRS wage and income transcript can sometimes help when you no longer have the original W-2. Taxpayers with an IRS Individual Online Account may be able to view available wage and income transcript information.

A transcript is not always the same as having the original W-2. For example, IRS transcript information does not include the state and local tax information reported on the original form. Current-year data may also be incomplete if the employer's information has not yet reached the IRS. For those reasons, the employer remains the best first place to request the actual form.

What If You Must File Before the W-2 Arrives?

A missing W-2 does not mean the wages can simply be ignored. The IRS states that taxpayers should still file on time and provides Form 4852 for situations in which an employer does not provide a W-2 or provides an incorrect one.

If Form 4852 is appropriate, accurate payroll records become important. A final pay stub can help estimate wages and withholding. The estimate should be based on real records rather than guesses. If the actual W-2 later arrives and differs from what was reported, an amended return using Form 1040-X may be required.

Keep Your Final Pay Stub When You Leave a Job

Saving your final pay stub is a useful habit whenever you change employers. The statement may show year-to-date wages, federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare taxes and other payroll information.

A pay stub does not replace an official W-2, but it can help you check the W-2 when it arrives and provide a record of your earnings if you later need to address a missing form. Downloading important payroll records before your employee account is disabled can save time months later.

If the Former Employer Has Closed

If the business has closed, start with any payroll records and contact information you still have. Old pay stubs, emails and payroll-provider notices may help you identify who handled the company's payroll.

If you cannot obtain the W-2 from the former employer or payroll provider, use the IRS procedures for missing documents. The goal is to report your actual wage information accurately, even when obtaining the original form is difficult.

A Practical Order to Follow

First, check the payroll portal and your email. Second, contact the former employer and verify your mailing address. Third, request a reissued copy when necessary. If the form remains missing, follow current IRS guidance rather than leaving the wages off your return.

The earlier you notice a missing W-2, the more time you have to solve the problem before the filing deadline.
