Why Corrected W-2 Forms Matter (Form W-2c Explained)

Form W-2c fixes a wage statement that went out wrong. What triggers one, what it means for a return you have already filed and why a wrong SSN is the most urgent error of all.

By Alexander Caldwell · September 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Key takeaways

  • Form W-2c is the corrected version of a W-2 that reported wrong wages, taxes or identifying details.
  • Employers file it with the SSA and furnish a copy to the employee.
  • A wrong Social Security number is the most urgent error because earnings stop being credited to your record.
  • If you already filed using the wrong figures, an amended return may be needed.
  • Compare the W-2c against the original before refiling, since only some boxes usually change.

Payroll departments process millions of wage statements in January, and some go out wrong. Form W-2c is the mechanism for fixing them: a corrected Wage and Tax Statement that supersedes the original. Understanding what it does determines whether you need to act on your tax return or simply file it away.

What Triggers a Correction

Common causes include a mistyped Social Security number or name, wages reported in the wrong box, a benefit coded incorrectly so that taxable wages were overstated or understated, and amounts assigned to the wrong tax year. Employers file Form W-2c with the Social Security Administration, usually alongside a corrected transmittal, and furnish a copy to the employee.

Why a Wrong SSN Is the Urgent One

An incorrect wage figure affects one tax year. An incorrect Social Security number affects your earnings record, which determines future Social Security benefits. If the number on your W-2 is wrong, raise it with payroll immediately rather than waiting for tax season to end, and check that the correction has actually been filed.

What It Means for a Return You Already Filed

If the corrected figures change your income, withholding or tax, the filed return no longer matches what the IRS has on record, and an amended return may be required. If the correction is cosmetic, for example a misspelled name with identical figures, there is often nothing to amend.

Work it out by comparing the two forms side by side. A W-2c typically shows previously reported amounts next to corrected ones, so the changed boxes are easy to isolate. I explain how to read that comparison and decide whether to amend in my explainer on corrected W-2 forms for Online Paystub.

If the Employer Will Not Correct It

Contact payroll in writing and keep the response. If an employer does not issue a correction after you have tried to resolve it, current IRS guidance sets out a process for requesting assistance. Do not simply enter different numbers on your return to produce the result you believe is right; use accurate records and document your attempts to obtain the corrected statement.
