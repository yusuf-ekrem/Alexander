Do Paychecks Expire? How Long a Paycheck Stays Valid

A paper paycheck does not vanish, but banks can refuse it after six months and unclaimed wages eventually go to the state. What the 180-day rule means and what to do with an old check.

By Alexander Caldwell · September 8, 2026 · 2 min read

Key takeaways

  • Under the Uniform Commercial Code a bank is not obliged to honour a check more than six months old.
  • Stale-dated does not mean void; the wages are still owed to you.
  • Many employers void old payroll checks and reissue rather than risk a double payment.
  • Unclaimed wages are eventually turned over to the state under escheatment rules.
  • Direct deposit sidesteps the problem entirely, which is why most employers prefer it.

A paycheck sitting in a drawer raises a reasonable question: is it still good? The short answer is that the check may stop being accepted long before the money stops being yours. Those are two different things, and confusing them is what causes most of the panic.

The Six-Month Rule

Under the Uniform Commercial Code, a bank is not required to pay a check presented more than six months after its date. Note the wording: not required. Some banks will still process a stale-dated check, others will refuse it outright, and the decision often depends on the account and the amount. You cannot rely on either outcome.

Stale Is Not the Same as Void

A refused check does not cancel the underlying obligation. The employer owed you wages for work performed, and that debt does not disappear because a piece of paper aged. What changes is the mechanism: instead of depositing the check, you ask the employer to void it and reissue payment.

Most payroll departments would rather do this than have an old check surface unexpectedly. From their side, an outstanding check is a liability sitting on the books and a risk of paying twice.

What Happens If Nobody Claims It

Unclaimed wages do not stay with the employer forever. State unclaimed property laws require holders to report and remit abandoned funds after a dormancy period, a process known as escheatment. The money then sits with the state, and the employee, or their heirs, can claim it through the state's unclaimed property office. It is recoverable, but it is a far slower route than asking payroll for a reissue.

The practical lesson is to deal with an uncashed check promptly rather than leaving it to the system. I cover the reissue process, the employer's side of the obligation and the state-by-state variation in my full explainer on whether paychecks expire for Online Paystub.

What to Do With an Old Check

Contact your employer's payroll department, tell them the check number and date, and ask them to void and reissue it. Do not attempt to deposit an old check at the same time, since a reissue plus a cleared original creates an overpayment you will be asked to return. Keep your pay stub for the period, because it documents what you were owed independently of the check itself.
